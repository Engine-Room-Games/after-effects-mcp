# Fragile areas — the bridge, the panel and CEP

The HTTP/WS bridge between the MCP server and the CEP panel, port discovery,
CEP's own quirks, and the screenshot pipeline that rides on top of them.

[server and packaging](fragile-areas-server.md).

## The five bridge failures, whose remedies contradict each other

- **A busy AE is indistinguishable from a dead bridge at the HTTP layer.** ExtendScript is single-threaded, so while a script runs — or a modal dialog sits unclicked — the panel cannot service its socket at all. The connection is accepted and then nothing comes back. That is why `httpClient` separates `BridgeTimeoutError` from `BridgeUnreachableError` and why `check_setup`'s `bridgeReachable` reports a timeout differently from a refusal: the two have opposite remedies, and "restart After Effects" said to someone whose script is still running throws away work for nothing. Enumerating `app.effects` (~250 entries, tens of seconds in 26.3) is the reproducible case — issue #26 — which is also why `list_available_effects` caches for the session. Never collapse the two errors back into one sentence.
- **There are now three of those, not two, and the third one's advice is the opposite of the second's.** `WriteQueueWaitError` means the call sat behind another write for the full `AE_MCP_WRITE_QUEUE_WAIT_MS` and was dropped **without ever leaving the server**. `BridgeTimeoutError` forbids re-sending, because that call did reach After Effects and may still be running; the queue error has to say the opposite, because nothing was written and re-sending once the queue drains is the correct move. `BridgeUnreachableError` sends the reader to `check_setup`, which the queue error must not, since the bridge is answering perfectly well. Three diagnoses, three remedies, no shared sentences — `tests/unit/write-queue.mjs` asserts they stay distinct, including that neither bridge error ever mentions the queue.
- **There is a fourth, and it is the only one where the panel says no on purpose.** `BridgeAuthError` means the panel answered — immediately, not slowly — and refused the call because the bridge token did not match. It shares nothing with the other three: the connection is not lost, so `check_setup` is not the answer; the panel is not busy, so waiting changes nothing; and unlike the queue error, re-sending is *not* safe in the sense of being useful — it will be refused identically until the token is fixed. `sentToken` splits the remedy in two and they point opposite ways: no token found means this server cannot see the file the panel wrote (a different home directory — a sandboxed client, another user), so look at where the panel logged it; a token found and rejected means the file belongs to a panel that has gone, so quit After Effects fully and reopen. `tests/unit/bridge-auth.mjs` asserts the four messages share no line of substance, the same way `write-queue.mjs` does for the first three.
- **A reset is a fifth, and it sits between a refusal and a timeout (issue #163).** `BridgeResetError` means the panel accepted the connection and then dropped it before answering — `ECONNRESET`, or undici's `UND_ERR_SOCKET` ("other side closed"), matched by code on the `cause` chain, never by text. `EPIPE` is the exception, checked first by `isUnsentError`: it is a failed *write* — usually onto a keep-alive socket the panel had already dropped — and the panel runs an op only on the request's `end` event, so a request whose write failed cannot have run; that one is classified unreachable. A reset proves nothing about delivery (fetch does not say whether the body was written or the socket reused), so it stays may-have-run. Until #163 it fell through to `BridgeUnreachableError`, which is the one failure `runOp` re-sends after rediscovery — and a reset is a request that *reached* a panel and may have run, so re-sending it is the #43 duplicate. Nor is it a timeout: nothing is still running, so "wait and poll" is wrong too. Its remedy is its own: do not re-send blindly, confirm the panel with `check_setup`, read the comp back to learn whether the call landed. `tests/unit/hardening-163.mjs` asserts it shares no line with the other four and that a reset is never re-sent to another port. The `after-effects` guide lists it beside the other agent-facing failures.
- **The server's op timeout must sit above the panel's own waits, not on them.** `saveFrameToPng` is asynchronous and the panel polls for the PNG for up to 120s; with the server also at 120s it gave up at the exact moment the panel might still have succeeded. `SLOW_OPS` in `bridge/httpClient.ts` gives 300s to the ops whose duration the caller chooses, After Effects does, or the project does: the two screenshot ops, `run_jsx`, `run_batch`, `export_mogrt`, `import_footage`, `place_audio_cues`, `purge_unused_footage` (one `usedIn` per footage item, over a bin that has reached 1,863) and `render_queue`'s prepare and save (a big .aep saves slowly; the render itself never crosses the bridge). `AE_MCP_OP_TIMEOUT_MS` overrides every op, deliberately including the slow ones — one number a user can reason about beats a matrix they cannot see.
- **A sixth sender of failures: the front desk (issue #223), which must add no new kind.** When sessions share a desk, a follower's bridge failure happens in *another process*. The desk sends it back as the same class with the leader's words verbatim (`frontdesk/protocol.ts`, round-tripped in `tests/unit/front-desk.mjs`), so the five stay five. The desk's own death or stall is the one new way a call can fail, and it is not a new kind. A write is re-sent only on proof it was never forwarded (refused connection, refusal status, `lost`); any other loss is a `FrontDeskLostError`, which *is* a `BridgeResetError` — may have run, read back first — and a read is re-sent. A desk that stalls and wakes must not forward what its requester gave up on: every call carries a deadline shorter than the requester's stall threshold, checked right before forwarding (issue #223's SIGSTOP double write). Never key that decision on a line the desk wrote, such as `start`: the desk can post to the panel and be killed before the line's bytes arrive, and re-sending then is a double write. A write that never left because its place in the queue was lost is a `DeskPlaceLostError`, which *is* a `WriteQueueWaitError` — nothing ran, re-send. Its message drops the reset's "confirm the panel with `check_setup`", because the panel never left; it was the other server that did. Never let a desk failure fall through to `BridgeUnreachableError`: `runOp` re-sends those, and a write that reached After Effects through a desk that then died is exactly the duplicate #43 is about.
- **A wait measured inside the panel is a lie, and only the desk can measure it.** While ExtendScript runs, the panel's event loop does not turn, so a timestamp taken there when a request arrives is taken *after* the wait it was meant to measure (0–2 ms read against seconds, AE 26.5). The desk is a Node process outside After Effects; its clock runs while AE is blocked. Anything that reports how long a session waited must be timed there.

- **After a timeout, the next call is refused unsent while After Effects is still busy — never sent to wait out a second limit.** Before this, a user holding After Effects (an open menu, a modal dialog, a text layer being typed into, a RAM preview, their own render) turned every call into its own 120 s hang, and each of those calls still ran once AE let go: late, out of order, after its agent had been told it timed out. Now `HttpClient` (`bridge/httpClient.ts`) keeps the earliest call it sent that timed out with no answer since (`stuck`), and before the next `/op` makes one quick `/health` (`BUSY_PROBE_MS`, 1.5 s, shared by every call that arrives while it runs; a `/health` that went unanswered within the last 2.5 s — the gate's own, say — is reused). Silence is busy: ExtendScript holds the panel's JavaScript thread for a whole script, measured to leave `/health` unanswered (the next section). An answer carrying `evalBusy` (the script the panel handed `evalScript` and has no reply for) is busy too — that covers a host that keeps CEP's thread running while AE itself is held, which is **not measured**: whether a menu, a dialog, a text edit or a RAM preview blocks CEP's thread or only `evalScript` is exactly what the live recipe has to record. An idle answer, or an `evalBusy` that started after the stuck call gave up (so it cannot be that call — the panel drops a queued call whose request closed), drops the record and the call goes out. Any other answer to an op clears the record too, since the panel runs calls one at a time in order; a refused probe proves nothing and the op goes out to meet the ordinary unreachable path. **The refusal is `AeBusyError`, a `WriteQueueWaitError`** — nothing left the server, re-sending is safe — and never `BridgeUnreachableError`, which `runOp` would re-send. It lives in `HttpClient` rather than `server.ts` because every op this process forwards passes there, other sessions' included when it runs the front desk, so a follower is refused by the leader's record and gets the same class back over the wire (`"ae-busy"` in `frontdesk/protocol.ts`). Its words are its own; `tests/unit/busy-guard.mjs` holds that, and the fast refusal.
- **The panel drops a queued call the server has already given up on.** Every `/op` body carries `timeoutMs`, the request's own limit, relative (`postOp`). The panel starts that span on a monotonic clock (`performance.now`) when the request arrives — after the server started its timer, so the panel's deadline is never earlier than the server's, and no wall-clock change or laptop sleep can move it. When a queued call's turn comes, `evalScript` asks its `gone()`: past that deadline by more than `DEADLINE_GRACE_MS` (1 s), the server has certainly reported it as timed out, so it is dropped unrun, logged ("`<op>` was not run, the MCP server's deadline for it passed …"), and its response destroyed rather than answered. That keeps "re-send only on proof it never reached the panel" intact: a dropped call is only ever one its caller was told may still land. The cost of timing from arrival: a request the panel could not even read while ExtendScript held its thread is timed from when it was read, so the request closing (`res` `close` before an answer, checked after one `setTimeout(0)`) is the signal for that case. A call already handed to `evalScript` is never touched. A request with no `timeoutMs` (an older server) is never dropped by time. `/health` reports `evalBusy` (`{op, ms}` or null) and `evalQueued`. Pinned in `tests/unit/panel-boot.mjs`.
- **"EvalScript error." from an op is most often After Effects declining to run the line.** Every op is sent as one fixed `__AEMCP.call(...)` line whose handler catches what an op throws and answers `{ok:false}`, so the bare string usually means AE would not run it, while held as above. The panel codes it `EVAL_SCRIPT_ERROR` for an op; `HttpClient` rewords it (`evalRefusedText`): very probably nothing ran, read back first — except `run_jsx`/`run_batch`, where the caller's own code can escape the handler part way through, worded as "may have partly run". The bundle load (no op label) keeps "ExtendScript eval failed (syntax or runtime)": there it is a parse or runtime error in the file. An `AeError` with a code, so presented without the `AE:` prefix.
- **A current gate verdict survives a timed-out probe for the full healthy interval.** `panelGate.ts` used to re-probe every 5 s while After Effects was busy, and each call the probe landed on paid its 2 s. A busy AE is still running the panel it was a moment ago, so a verdict last seen current now waits `HEALTHY_RECHECK_MS` (60 s); a stale verdict, or none yet, keeps the 5 s retry; a refused probe still drops everything.

## Two MCP clients, one panel (issue #175)

Two AI clients — two MCP server processes — can point at the same panel, and nothing stops them. Each server has its own write queue (`bridge/writeQueue.ts`) and knows nothing of the other's; what they share is After Effects. **Measured on 26.5**, two server processes against one panel:

- **While a `run_jsx` script runs, the panel answers nothing at all — not even `/health`.** ExtendScript blocks the CEP JavaScript thread for the script's whole duration, so client Y's request is not even *read* until client X's script returns. Calls never interleave, but for that reason, not because of the panel's `evalScript` mutex: by the time Y's request is read, the mutex is idle.
- **The panel's mutex cannot measure that wait.** A first version of this change timed the wait on the panel's mutex and reported it as `panelBusyWith` / `panelWaitedMs`. Live, Y really waited 5.3 s and the mutex wait read 2 ms (`{"behind":"run_jsx","waitedMs":2}`) — under the 1 s reporting threshold, so nothing was said. Behind a 600-op `run_batch`, `behind` read `run_batch` but the wait read 0–1 ms while Y had waited 57–225 ms. The feature was removed rather than shipped: a field that is silent exactly when the wait is long, and reads near zero when it does speak, is worse than none. (A panel log of which client each evaluation ran for, checked against the server's send time, could measure it; it was not built.) Reporting the other client is left to a shared broker process that every session goes through (separate work), which sees both clients' requests from the moment they are sent.
- **Another client's writes land between a long batch's chunks.** Y's null layers landed between batch layers B349/B350 and B549/B550 — on chunk boundaries. X's server holds its own lease across the batch, but the lease is per process; the panel drives the chunks one `evalScript` at a time and runs whatever request it reads in between. This was inferred from the code before; it is now measured.
- **A timeout while waiting behind the other client still lands.** With `AE_MCP_OP_TIMEOUT_MS=3000`, Y was told it timed out and its layer appeared afterwards. So "may still be running" in the timeout message is not a hedge: a call can time out before it has started, and runs when After Effects is free. Re-sending it would apply it twice.
- **The timeout fired at 5.0 s, not 3.** Y was a fresh server, so its first call asked `/health` for the panel-version gate (`bridge/panelGate.ts`) before sending the op — a 2 s probe (`HEALTH_TIMEOUT_MS`) that the blocked panel also cannot answer. The gate treats a probe timeout as "proceed", then the op's own 3 s clock starts. 2 + 3 = 5. The op clock is deliberately started at send, not at the tool call (see "Serializing writes" in architecture.md), so the fix is in the message, not the clock: `server.ts` times the call from before the gate, and `BridgeTimeoutError.withTiming()` restates the message as "gave up 5.0s after it was made; the op's own limit is 3s … the other 2.0s went on earlier steps of this call. Of that, 2.0s checking the panel's version" whenever the call outlasted its limit by half a second or more. Only the gate and this session's write queue are itemised, because only they are measured; `render_queue`'s prepare round trip before a timed-out save, or a refused attempt plus the port search before a re-send, count as "earlier steps" and nothing more, so the listed parts need not add up. Every timeout message also says the call may still land. `tests/unit/bridge-timeout.mjs` and `tests/unit/write-queue-server.mjs` pin both.

What none of this does is lock anything across clients. A cross-process write lock would have to live somewhere both clients go through and be held across a batch's chunks — which is the broker's job, not the panel's. That broker is the front desk (issue #223; "The front desk" in [architecture.md](architecture.md)), run by one of the servers.

## Ports and discovery

- **The port file is not the authority; the socket is.** `~/.engineroom-ae-mcp/port` is written by whichever panel bound *last*, and a panel that walked to 7780 and then died leaves 7780 there while the panel that is actually answering sits on 7777. Until 0.5.0 `HttpClient` read that file once, in its constructor, and posted every op there for the life of the process, while `check_setup` re-read it on every call — so setup passed and every op failed, four times in one week (issue #92). Two things fixed it and both are load-bearing. `discovery.ts` orders the candidates **7777 first, then the file**, because 7777 is where the panel binds unless something is wrong and the file is the thing that was wrong; and `runOp` re-runs discovery only when a call is **refused**. Refused means the request never reached a panel, so re-sending cannot run it twice; a timeout means it may still be running, and re-sending *that* is issue #43. The retry is a fresh `postOp` with its own `AbortSignal.timeout`, so a refused-then-found call never reports as slow, and it happens only when a *different* port answers as the panel — the same port answering again is the original error's story. `AE_MCP_PORT` pins the candidate list to one port and switches nothing, on purpose: a pin is how a second After Effects instance is addressed deliberately. `tests/unit/port-drift.mjs` runs all of it against stubs on ephemeral ports; nothing in it ever asks 7777, because on the machine this is developed on a real panel holds it.
- **A walked port is a zombie panel, not a busy port.** On one machine nothing but this panel ever binds 7777, so `EADDRINUSE` at startup means a previous instance of the panel is still there — a CEPHtmlEngine that lingered across an After Effects relaunch (a second panel *window* could do it too until #283; the status window binds nothing). Until 0.5.0 `startServers` walked up one port at a time to 7799 and wrote whatever it got to the port file, which routed silently around the zombie and manufactured the drift above. Now it asks `/health` on the held port first. If the answer is our own shape (`isOurHealth` — `ok` plus `bundleHash`, or `bundleLoaded` on a pre-0.3 panel; the same test as `isPanelHealth` in `discovery.ts`), it does **not** walk: the status reads "waiting — port 7777 is held by another AE MCP panel", the log names the holder's bundle hash and whether it matches, and the bind is retried every 2s so the new panel takes over the moment the zombie exits, with no restart. Anything else holding the port — or nothing answering — still walks, loudly, naming the holder and the port chosen, since that is the one case where the port file legitimately says something other than 7777. `allowPortWalk: true` in `~/.engineroom-ae-mcp/config.json` opts back into walking past our own panel for people who run two After Effects at once; `port` in the same file moves the panel, and `0` binds an ephemeral port, which is how `tests/unit/panel-boot.mjs` keeps every test panel off the real 7777. The unload hook removes the port file only if it still names this panel's port — another panel's entry is not ours to erase — and CEP does fire `unload` on a normal After Effects quit — measured 2026-09-08 on 26.3: the file was gone within a second of quitting, for a panel on 7777 and for one walked to 7778 (recipe 44).
- **The walk stops at 65535 and says so.** A walk from a held 65535 used to ask `listen()` for 65536, which throws a RangeError ("options.port should be >= 0 and < 65536") that names no port the user configured, and the panel showed "failed" with only that line. `walk()` now refuses the same way it does past `WALK_LIMIT` — "ports X through 65535 are all in use", plus "65535 is the highest port there is; set a lower "port" in config.json" — and writes no port file. Only a configured port near the top reaches it in real use; it surfaced in `tests/unit/panel-boot.mjs` when the ephemeral port a test panel drew was 65535 (issue #260), which is also why that test now keeps its port numbers in a table of its own rather than the machine's shared port space.
- **Under an `AE_MCP_PORT` pin, `check_setup` must ask more ports than ops do.** `HttpClient.candidates()` is `[pinned]` on purpose — a pin is never walked past — but a diagnosis that asks only that port reports "nothing is listening" for a panel that is fine on 7777 and sends the user to restart After Effects (recipe 43's pinned half failed exactly so on 2026-09-08). `server.ts` hands `check_setup` the op candidates plus `diagnosticPortCandidates()` (the default port and the port file, after the pin), `portAgreement` says the port is *pinned* rather than stale, and `buildNextSteps` branches on that word to say "change the pin" instead of "retry" — retrying a pinned port changes nothing. `tests/unit/port-drift.mjs` holds all three.

## The bridge token

- **Loopback is not a security boundary, and `/op` is an `eval` inside After Effects.** Issue #106, reported by a user who read `main.js` before installing it. Any page in any browser can POST to `127.0.0.1` without asking anyone; `/op` reaches `run_jsx`, which is an unrestricted `eval`, and ExtendScript has `File`, `Folder` and `system.callSystem` — so an unauthenticated bridge is arbitrary code execution as the user, offered to every site they visit. The panel had `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on every response as well, which added the ability to read the results back.
- **Dropping the CORS header would not have fixed it, and that is the part worth remembering.** A cross-origin POST with `content-type: text/plain` is a CORS *simple request*: no preflight is sent, so there is no preflight to fail, the browser delivers it, and the panel — which never inspected content-type — parses the body as JSON and runs the op. The attacker never reads the response and never needs to. CORS governs who may *read* an answer, never whether a side effect happens, so nothing expressed in CORS terms can close this. The `application/json` shape *is* blocked today, incidentally rather than by design: it forces a preflight, and `OPTIONS` falls into the `405 POST only` branch. That is a speed bump, not a lock, and `tests/unit/bridge-auth.mjs` fires the text/plain shape specifically so a future refactor that gates on content-type instead of the token fails loudly.
- **The token works because a web page cannot read a file.** The panel mints 32 random bytes per session and writes them to `~/.engineroom-ae-mcp/token-<port>`, mode 0600, *inside `bindWith` before the bind promise resolves* — the socket is already accepting by then, so writing it later in the boot would leave a window where a real call is refused. Per port, not one file per machine: the port file is only a hint (issue #92), and two After Effects instances under `allowPortWalk` each hold their own, so a server that has found a panel on a port must be able to read *that* panel's token. The unload hook removes it under the same rule as the port file — only if it still holds this panel's own token.
- **Every route that reaches After Effects is gated; `/health` is deliberately not.** `/op`, `/cancel`, `/reload-jsx` and the `/events` WebSocket all check, before the request body is read — an unidentified caller must not get the process to buffer anything for it. `/health` stays open because discovery asks it before it knows which panel is there, and because a second panel asks it to find out who holds its port (issue #92); it carries nothing a page could use, and its `auth: true` flag is what lets `check_setup` tell "needs a token" from "too old to want one". A panel from before #106 omits the flag entirely, which is the whole compatibility story: a new server sends a header an old panel ignores.
- **`/reload-jsx` refuses while a batch is running (issue #163).** The ExtendScript job table lives in the bundle's closure, so a reload drops every chunked `run_batch` in flight; measured on AE 26.5, a reload sent mid-batch waited its turn on the evalScript chain and the batch then stopped at "225 of 1200 attempted" with those ops applied. The panel tracks the jobs it is driving (`jobsInFlight` in `client/main.js`) and answers 409 `batch_in_flight`, naming each job and its progress. `POST /reload-jsx?force=1` reloads anyway, and the batch then reports exactly what had landed. The dev command is `make jsx` (`make jsx FORCE=1` to override), or `curl -X POST -H "x-ae-mcp-token: $(cat ~/.engineroom-ae-mcp/token-7777)" http://127.0.0.1:7777/reload-jsx` by hand — add `?force=1` to the URL to override. `tests/unit/hardening-163.mjs` covers the refusal and the override.
- **The `Origin` check is depth, and only depth.** Browsers attach `Origin` to every cross-origin request and cannot be talked out of it; Node's `fetch` and the `ws` client send none (measured — undici sets `sec-fetch-mode` but no `Origin`). So a request carrying one is from a web page by definition and is refused whatever token it has. It is not what holds — the token is — but it costs one line and it fails closed.
- **The WebSocket needed this most.** The same-origin policy does not apply to WebSockets at all, so `/events` was open to any page regardless of CORS; a page could connect and watch the user's session. It is also where a header is close to sufficient on its own, because a browser cannot set one on a WebSocket. `verifyClient` refuses the upgrade with a 403 rather than accepting and closing.

## CEP and the panel

- **The bridge is an invisible extension; the window is a second extension that runs nothing (issue #283).** Until then the bridge was a `Panel` with `AutoVisible=true`, because `AutoVisible=false` on a *Panel* was unreliable in early CEP 12 builds — so a window opened at every After Effects launch, and closing it unloaded the bridge. The manifest now carries two extensions in the one bundle. `games.engine-room.ae-mcp.bridge` is `Type=Custom`, `AutoVisible=false`, no `<Menu>`, a 1×1 `Size` (CEP requires one), Node through `--enable-nodejs --mixed-context`, started by both `com.adobe.csxs.events.ApplicationActivate` and `applicationActivate` — the cookbook lists one per host, and the first is what started the old panel in AE. **Measured on AE 26.5:** it starts at launch (`/health` in 7–11 s), no window appears, tools work, and the status extension is not started. `games.engine-room.ae-mcp.panel` — the id the visible bridge had — is now the `Panel` under Window > Extensions > AE MCP Bridge: no Node, no `StartOn`, a page (`client/status.html` + `status.js`) that never loads `main.js`, so opening it, or two of it, cannot bind a port. The id moved on purpose: a workspace saved with the old panel docked names `…ae-mcp.panel` in that frame, and keeping a `Panel` behind the name means the upgrade restores the status window into the frame rather than a `Panel` slot naming a hidden extension, which nobody has measured. `check_setup`'s CEP-log match is on the bundle id, so it covers both. The window shows what main.js mirrors to it over an application-scoped CEP event (`games.engine-room.ae-mcp.event.status` — event types are their own namespace, never an extension id — the whole status and log, at most every 250 ms, and at once on `…event.status-request`). **Silence is not death:** while ExtendScript runs a long op the answers can stop for as long as it runs, so a quiet bridge reads "no answer for Ns (After Effects may be busy)", with the last port and log kept, and the window returns to the bridge's own status on the next answer. Only a bridge it has *never* heard from gets `requestOpenExtension` on the bridge id, once — a no-op for a running extension, and the recovery for a host that did not start it. `tests/unit/panel-manifest.mjs` holds the shape and status.js's behaviour; `panel-boot.mjs` holds the mirror. **The fallback if a host does not start the hidden extension:** give the bridge back its old `Lifecycle`/`UI` (`Type=Panel`, `AutoVisible=true`, `<Menu>AE MCP Bridge</Menu>`, a 320×200 size) under the `.panel` id and drop the status extension — `index.html` still renders the status itself. A middle step worth trying first is adding `<StartOn>` with the activate event to the *status* panel: its `requestOpenExtension` then starts the bridge at launch, at the cost of the window this issue removes.
- CEP panels installed without signing require `PlayerDebugMode=1`. The user does this once via `npm run enable:debug` and a reboot. Except when it is not enough — see the CEP 12 signature bullet below. Since issue #182 a release ships the panel signed, so this applies to the dev flow and to builds packed without a certificate; see the signed-panel bullet.
- **A signed panel is a list of every file, and CEP holds the folder to it.** Measured against `ZXPSignCmd -verify` 4.1.3 (the library CEP loads): one extra file fails verification, one changed byte fails it, a missing `mimetype` fails it; `.DS_Store` at any depth and empty directories are ignored. Three consequences. `ws` goes inside the panel *before* signing, because `setup_panel` adding it afterwards — the unsigned path's normal step — voids the signature. The signed folder must contain no symlinks: Adobe's 2024 known issue is installers turning links into text files, and the packaging copy already dereferences. And anything that writes into an installed panel — `AE_MCP_SYNC_PANEL`, a hand edit for `/reload-jsx` — breaks a signed install, which then loads only through PlayerDebugMode; `check_setup`'s `panelSignatureIntact` says so, from the digests in `signatures.xml`, without Adobe's tool. What it cannot check is the RSA signature or CEP's acceptance of it: that needs live After Effects (recipe 76). Adobe also advises signing on the platform the panel will run on; the release signs everything on macOS, and whether CEP on Windows accepts that is the main thing recipe 76 exists to find out.
- **CEP 12 enforces signature verification despite PlayerDebugMode on some Windows installs, and only the CEP log says so.** The panel never loads, no CEPHtmlEngine.exe ever starts, nothing appears in AE, nothing is in the Windows event log, and `check_setup` reported every check green with the bridge refused — so it sent the user round "restart After Effects" indefinitely (issue #91; a matching report exists against Premiere on macOS, so it is not Windows-only in principle). The one line of evidence, `ERROR Signature verification failed for extension games.engine-room.ae-mcp.panel`, is in CEP's own log — `%TEMP%\CEP12-AEFT.log` as measured, `~/Library/Logs/CSXS/` on macOS, `csxs<n>-AEFT.log` per Adobe's cookbook, so `cepLogPaths()` matches both prefixes — and only once `LogLevel` is set. Three consequences. `check_setup` reads the newest log (last 256KB, so a level-6 log cannot stall it) whenever AE is running, the panel is installed and the bridge is *refused*, and a hit becomes `panelSignature` with the ZXPSignCmd self-signing steps as `nextSteps`, restart last; with no log it says how to turn logging on instead of asking for a third restart. `installPanel` sets `LogLevel` to 6 — the value measured to record the line; lower ones are undocumented — for every CSXS version where it was **never set**, leaving a present value alone even if it is 0, so the evidence exists next time. And the signed folder carries `META-INF/` and `mimetype`, which `panelInstallDiff` must go on ignoring and `setup_panel` must warn about before it strips them (`signedInstallOverwritten`). The Windows half of this has never been exercised by anyone on the project — recipe 45.
- **A current panel sends no job events.** Since issue #220 stage 5 the server drives long batches itself, and since #243 the panel's own driving loop is gone, so a current panel broadcasts nothing on `/events`. `wsClient.ts` still turns job events into `JobManager` updates, for one case: a panel older than #220 that slips past the gate and drives its own batch, which the server then follows so `await_job` can say when it has finished. The socket, the panel's `ws` dependency and `wsClient.ts` also stay for a second reason: the socket dropping and reopening is how the server notices After Effects was restarted on the same port, which is what drops the panel gate's stale verdict (`WsClient.onReopen`, issue #156). Remove the socket and that signal goes with it; the gate would then wait out its recheck interval instead.
- **The panel cannot start without `ws`, and cannot say so.** `main.js` requires it at boot; before this was fixed, a failed require threw out of the top-level IIFE before the DOM handles existed, so the panel sat on "starting…" for ever and the only symptom was silence on port 7777. The require now runs *after* the logger is set up and bails out visibly. Keep it in that order.
- **A panel install can be half-written, and it looks fine.** Installing while AE holds the client files open updates `jsx/bundle.jsx` and fails on the rest. `panelUpToDate` used to hash only the bundle, so it went green on a mix of two versions while every call failed — and because the bundle *was* current, `assessPanel` concluded `restart-needed` and sent the user round a loop no restart could end. `panelInstallDiff()` compares every shipped file, and `installComplete: false` outranks the restart verdict. Reported as issue #20.
- **CEP anchors `__dirname` at the extension root, not at the folder holding the file.** So `require("ws")` resolves (node_modules is at the root) while `require("./pngcodec.js")` from `client/main.js` did not — it looks for `<ext>/pngcodec.js`, which is not where the file is. This is why the panel is the only part of the system whose bootstrap has to be tested rather than reasoned about: it shipped on `versions/0.3.0` refusing to start with "cannot start — pngcodec.js … is missing", and nothing caught it, because there is no AE on a runner, the unit tests require those modules by absolute path, and the one machine it had ever run on still had a pre-#36 panel installed. **Resolve panel-internal paths from `cs.getSystemPath(SystemPath.EXTENSION)`, never from `__dirname`** — that is authoritative, and `main.js` now builds `clientDir` from it before requiring anything (today that is only `panelhash.js`; the image modules moved to the server in #220 and were deleted from the panel in #243). `tests/unit/panel-boot.mjs` runs the real `main.js` against a stub CEP host in a copy of the installed layout and asserts it reaches a listening `/health`.
- **CEP returns a file URL, not a path.** `getSystemPath` gives `file:///C:/Users/…` on Windows, so stripping only the scheme leaves `/C:/…`; `path.join` then reads it as root-relative and produces `\C:\…\bundle.jsx`, and the panel reports the bundle missing while it sits at that exact location. `client/csinterface.js` strips the slash before a drive letter — do that there, not at call sites, since it is the one place a URL becomes a native path. `tests/unit/panel-paths.mjs` covers it; there is no AE on a runner, so nothing else does.


## Screenshots and frame integrity

- **The image code lives on the server, once (#220 stage 4, #243).** Everything after `saveFrameToPng` — the completeness wait, normalising, the stale check, the contact sheet, the `.mogrt` thumbnail — is done by the server (`packages/mcp-server/src/frames/`). The server sends `_screenshot_frame` / `_screenshot_layer` / `_export_mogrt`, which the panel passes to the bundle untouched, so the panel returns ExtendScript's temp paths and no image crosses the bridge as base64. Until #243 the panel kept its own copy of all of it for older servers, and `tests/unit/image-parity.mjs` held the two to identical bytes; the panel's copy is gone, the version gate keeps an older panel from being asked, and an `Unknown op:` for one of these is answered with the update message rather than re-sent under the public name. What moved changed shape in two ways. A retry is a second `/op` (a read, so it queues behind nothing) rather than a second `evalScript` inside one; the 120s frame budget and the contact sheet's 240s deadline run on the server, inside the screenshot op's own 300s ceiling per `/op`. And the `.mogrt` rewrite happens after `_export_mogrt` has returned, still inside `export_mogrt`'s call, so its write lease covers the patch.

- `saveFrameToPng` is community-known, not officially documented. Alpha edge cases reported on some comps. If it fails, fallback would be the render queue with PNG Sequence template (slow; deferred to v1.1).
- **`saveFrameToPng` is asynchronous, and "the file stopped growing" is not the same statement as "the file is finished".** It returns before the bytes are on disk, so anything reading the PNG has to decide for itself when the write is done. Until 0.4.0 that decision was *settling*: two `stat` calls 30ms apart reporting the same size. What that let through is every writer pause longer than 30ms — which on a heavy comp (the report was ~88 layers, a full-frame background precomp plus several shot precomps) is routine. The panel read the file mid-flight, shipped it, and the agent got `truncated PNG: chunk IDAT runs past the end of the file` for a render that was still happening. Completion is now *structural*: the file is finished when it ends in a zero-length IEND chunk and every chunk length from the signature adds up to exactly that (`pngCodec.inspectPngStructure`), so a partial write cannot be delivered at all — only waited on. `frames/frameReader.ts` polls for that, with a cheap tail probe in front of the full read because IEND is the last chunk in the format and a file that does not end in one does not need reading. Three numbers: 120s of render budget (a cold 4K render was measured over 15 seconds; the original 5s silently failed screenshots that were merely still rendering), a 6s stall window after which a file that has stopped changing and still is not a PNG is declared abandoned, and one automatic re-render. The retry is safe because both screenshot ops are read-only and leave nothing in the project — unlike `run_jsx`, where re-running duplicates side effects (#43) — and it fires **only** on a corrupt read, never on a timeout: a timeout has already spent the 120s budget and a second one would push the op past the server's 300s ceiling, turning a precise diagnosis into a bridge timeout with the opposite remedy. `growable` in `inspectPngStructure` is what keeps the fast cases fast: a wrong signature or a missing IHDR can never be fixed by more bytes, so it fails immediately instead of waiting out the stall. Reported as issue #45.
- **A failed frame read must never be cached, and the two failures must never share a message.** The other half of #45 was the diagnosis, not the read: the old passthrough path hashed the *truncated* bytes into the frame cache (then the panel's `framecache.js`), so the next truncation at the same byte count came back as `Stale frame` — the reported symptom was "the same 73,877 bytes for different times and downsamples". Only a frame that decoded and was delivered reaches `frameCache.remember` now. And `FRAME_INCOMPLETE` ("the file After Effects wrote is not a whole PNG"; retry at a higher downsample) and `RENDER_TIMEOUT` ("it did not finish in time"; wait, do not retry immediately) get completely separate messages, for the same reason `BridgeTimeoutError` and `BridgeUnreachableError` do: the remedies point in opposite directions. Neither message may ever suggest restarting After Effects or re-running `setup_panel`, and `tests/unit/frame-integrity.mjs` asserts that.
- **`saveFrameToPng` re-serves stale buffers and reports success.** Past some per-frame render cost, AE hands back a frame it rendered earlier: byte-identical results for unrelated comps at unrelated times, and at *different* `downsample` factors, which cannot even be the same number of pixels. Nothing in the response distinguishes it — fresh temp file, `ok:true`, well-formed PNG — so content identity is the only signal available. `frames/frameCache.ts` keeps the last 24 delivered frames keyed by `(op, compId, layerId, time, downsample)` and refuses any frame whose pixels match a *different* key. It is an error rather than a warning on the image because an agent that can see the picture believes the picture. The window has to be **one per After Effects**, not one per caller: the bug is AE's, and it can hand one session a buffer it rendered for another — with the front desk (#227) several sessions on one AE is the ordinary case. Until #220 stage 4 the panel's memory was that window. The server finishes frames now, so the window moved to a file per panel port, `~/.engineroom-ae-mcp/frames-<port>.jsonl`, shared by every server process on that port whether or not the desk is on (`frames/sharedFrameCache.ts`). A delivered frame is one appended line, and past 200 lines the file is compacted to the live window through a temp file and a rename; both writes are made under a short lock file, so a compaction cannot drop a line another server appended meanwhile (a lock held past 5s is a dead writer's and is broken; a record has one 0.5s deadline covering the lock and every retry under it; one that misses it — held, stale and unremovable, or unwritable — is kept by this process alone, as is every record for the next 60s without waiting, and it warns once on stderr, so a stuck lock costs one half-second wait a minute and never hangs the server). **On Windows, contention is not only `EEXIST`.** A lock its holder has just deleted stays on disk "delete pending" while any other process still has a handle on it — another server's `stat` of it is enough — and creating it again in that window fails `EPERM`. Measured on windows-latest (PR #241): three writers saw it on most rounds, and the first version, which treated anything but `EEXIST` as "cannot lock", silently dropped one writer's records into its own window — the flake in #239's CI. `EPERM`/`EBUSY`/`EACCES` at the lock are therefore waited out under the same deadline, and the lock's own release, the append, the compaction's read and its rename retry them too, within the same deadline. A compaction whose read fails skips itself: rewriting the window from a failed read would empty it. A check reads without the lock and replays the file through `frameCache.ts`'s own window logic at each line's time. **The window is one *running* After Effects, not one port:** AE can only re-serve a buffer from the process that rendered it, and the file outlives AE, so every line is tagged with a hash of the panel's per-session bridge token and lines from any other instance are ignored — without that, a restarted AE refused fresh renders for matching a dead session's. The file is set to 0600 on every append, because an append's mode does nothing to an existing file; the server also tightens `~/.engineroom-ae-mcp` to 0700, since the panel creates it with no mode (0755 under a usual umask) and everything in it is read and written only by the same user. On Windows neither applies and the profile directory's ACL is what protects it. An unreadable or unwritable file means this process's own window, never a failed screenshot. `tests/unit/frame-cache-shared.mjs` holds all of it with real processes. The panel's in-memory window went with its image code in #243, so a hand-rolled `POST /op` of a screenshot op gets ExtendScript's temp path back and no stale check at all; only the server's tool path is checked. Two consequences to keep in mind: the *first* stale buffer of a session always gets through, since there is nothing yet to compare it against; and a genuinely static comp screenshotted at two times does trip it, which is why the message says so and points at a different `downsample` to distinguish the two. Reported as issue #29. The contact-sheet path is the one place that second consequence is *resolved* rather than merely explained: inside one sheet the caller asked for several times deliberately, so a tile matching a sibling is flagged in its `note` and the picture is still sent. The relaxation is scoped to a sheet's own tiles and nothing else — a single-frame call, and a sheet tile matching anything from outside the sheet, are both still refused, and `tests/unit/screenshot-pipeline.mjs` asserts the detector still fires.
- **A 16-bit project renders 16-bit-per-channel PNGs, and many decoders reject those outright.** `frames/pngCodec.ts` converts to 8 bits per channel before the server base64-encodes; an 8-bit frame with no partly transparent pixel is passed through byte-for-byte and never re-encoded. Taking the high byte of each 16-bit sample is exact rather than lossy-with-drift, because 8→16 promotion multiplies by 257. The same pass reports a frame whose every pixel is transparent as `empty: true` with **no image** — the ~5KB PNG that encodes one is the other thing decoders choke on, and "the frame is empty" is the useful reading anyway. `tests/unit/png-codec.mjs` and `tests/unit/frame-cache.mjs` cover both with synthetic fixtures and a second, independent PNG implementation; CI runs them, because there is no 16-bit AE project on a runner and this is real image code.
- **`saveFrameToPng` writes premultiplied colour into a PNG, and PNG is straight alpha.** Every soft edge — blur, feather, glow, shadow, anti-aliasing — decoded with a dark fringe that is not in the comp, at 8 and 16 bits alike (issue #130, measured on AE 2026). `normalizePng` divides it out (`c * 255 / a`, clamped; `a = 0` and `a = max` untouched), at 16-bit precision *before* narrowing — after narrowing, a 16-bit alpha of 300 is an 8-bit alpha of 1 and its colour is noise. Three rules keep it exactly once: `normalizePng` returns the straight samples as `pixels` and the contact sheet tiles those without converting; `decodePng8` stays a raw decoder, so its two callers (the sheet's passthrough fallback and the `.mogrt` poster) convert explicitly; and the `.mogrt` poster is box-filtered **while still premultiplied** — the alpha-weighted average, so clear pixels cannot bleed a halo in — and un-premultiplied only if the thumbnail keeps alpha, since dropping alpha from premultiplied colour is already the correct composite over black. The stale-frame hash (`hashInput`) is of the samples *before* un-premultiplying: the bug it detects is one AE buffer served twice, so the buffer is what it identifies, and the 16-bit division can merge two frames that differ only at a low-alpha edge. Normalising is therefore no longer idempotent — run it once, on After Effects' file, never on its own output.
